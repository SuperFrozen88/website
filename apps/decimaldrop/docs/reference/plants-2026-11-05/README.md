## Scope

**Science → Matter and Energy in Plants / Canopy Camp** has 120 original multiple-choice questions across 10 skills, 12 quick study cards, and 12 expandable review topics. It covers all 22 supplied images. Exact classroom quiz coverage is unconfirmed. Each question’s `photoNumbers` maps to the index above. Questions and summaries are original, not copied assessments.

- Photosynthesis: light energy becomes chemical energy stored in sugar; water and carbon dioxide inputs; oxygen output.
- Matter and structures: much plant matter comes from air and water; roots absorb water/minerals, stems transport, leaves capture light and exchange gases.
- Producers, consumers, and stored energy: plants make food for their own growth, repair and reproduction; food energy can pass to eaters.
- Hydroponics: soil-free growing with nutrient-rich water, support media, horticulture and small-space applications.
- Aquaponics: aquatic animals linked with hydroponic plant growing through water and nutrient flows.
- Epiphytes: support on other plants with supplies from the surrounding environment.
- Phytoremediation: uptake/storage or sometimes transformation of particular pollutants, with evidence and limits.
- Bottle investigation: wick, support medium, label-based nutrient directions, seed-packet instructions, and fair comparisons.

## Scientific distinctions and sources

Minerals are not the sugar plants make. Soil-free plants still need minerals and suitable light, water and air; plain water alone is not a complete indefinite system. Fish waste can supply nutrients through system processes but does not replace sunlight or photosynthesis. Not all orchids, mosses or bromeliads are epiphytes. A plant growing on a tree is not automatically a parasite. Plant-based cleanup is not universal or instant, and storing a pollutant is not necessarily destroying it. One drooping plant does not establish a single certain cause.

The photos are the primary scope source. Scientific checks: [NGSS Grade 5](https://www.nextgenscience.org/topic-arrangement/5matter-and-energy-organisms-and-ecosystems) (related standards 5-LS1-1 and 5-PS3-1), [UMN hydroponics](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/small-scale-hydroponics), [Cornell hydroponics/aquaponics](https://smallfarms.cornell.edu/resources/guide-to-urban-farming/hydroponics-and-aquaponics/), and [EPA phytoremediation](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2015-04/documents/phytoremprimer.pdf). These references clarify science, not the teacher’s exact quiz.
