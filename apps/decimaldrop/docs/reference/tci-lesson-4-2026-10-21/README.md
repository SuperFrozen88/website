## Source boundaries

- Use reading passages to establish facts and reading checks to identify learning targets. Entered student responses (especially image 6) are **not an authoritative answer key**.
- Use original study questions and concise original summaries. Preserve the supplied source captures as references rather than copying their assessments into the game.
- Avoid repeating broad textbook simplifications as universal facts: other Christian traditions existed in Europe in 1517; Granada’s conquest does not establish the complete political unification of modern Spain; the triangular-trade diagram is a simplified model, not every voyage’s itinerary.
- Crops are described as natural products rather than manufactured tools in this lesson. Botanical remains can still be archaeological evidence.
- The nine numbered sections are core reading. The two additional readings are visibly labeled **Read & Do** and **Explore**, and their skills start with independent evidence gaps. Readiness concerns the supplied practice scope; it does not certify a particular teacher’s quiz or independently assess a student’s written argument.
